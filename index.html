How to play

A nonogram is a grid where some cells are filled and the rest are empty. The numbers beside each row and above each column tell you the lengths of the runs of filled cells in that line, in order. Runs are separated by at least one empty cell.

Row 2 1 means: a run of two filled cells, then at least one gap, then a single filled cell. Column 4 means four in a row somewhere in that column.

Controls

  • Tap a cell to fill it. Tap again to clear it.
  • Drag along a row or column to paint several cells. A counter shows how many you've painted.
  • Switch to the X tool to mark cells you know are empty. Or long-press to use the other tool for that stroke.
  • Pinch to zoom and two-finger drag to pan on big boards. The clue numbers stay put while you scroll. The pan tool does the same with one finger.
  • Clue numbers grey out when their run is placed. When a line is complete, its leftover cells turn to X automatically (optional).
  • Stuck? Hint points at a mistake if you've made one, or reveals one cell a line can prove right now.

Solving tips

  • Overlap: a clue of 8 in a 10-wide row must cover the middle six cells no matter where it sits. Fill those first.
  • Edges: if a filled cell touches the border, its run extends inward by the clue length.
  • Mark the X's: cells that can't be filled are just as useful as ones that must be. Every puzzle here is solvable with logic alone.
  • Zero means the line is completely empty.

Slam mode

Beat the clock. Solve a puzzle and time is added; wrong fills cost time and reset your streak. Each clean solve raises the multiplier, and finishing fast earns a speed bonus. When the clock hits zero, the run is over and your score goes on the board.
